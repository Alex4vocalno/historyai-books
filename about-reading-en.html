The cost of reading

The cost of reading goes beyond a book's price

Exploring a new field rarely ends with buying one book.

You may need to compare explanations, fill in background knowledge or follow a note into another question. Each purchase decision can affect how far that exploration goes. Whether a book is affordable also depends on a reader's income and circumstances.

We respect the value of authorship, editing and publishing. Making reading easier is not a claim that this work should go unpaid. It is a search for another way to share the costs so that more public works can reach more people.

For EVORON, free reading is part of the current product model, not an introductory offer left for readers to discover later.

Free access

Make obtaining a book easier first

In the EVORON library, you can look through a description and table of contents before entering the text. Reading public works does not require buying writing credits.

You can try an unfamiliar subject or move to another book when a work is not right for you. There is no purchase to justify by continuing, and no need to abandon a book simply because you cannot yet judge its value.

Free access lowers the cost of trying and obtaining a work. Whether it deserves to be read further remains a question for the content itself.

Beyond price

Beyond free: discoverable, understandable and usable

Removing a price does not remove every barrier to reading.

Find it. Clear titles, descriptions, categories and contents help readers understand what a book addresses, beyond the appeal of its cover. We want works to be discovered for their content, not only because their authors already have an audience.

Understand it. Language and background knowledge shape reading. EVORON currently offers works in Chinese and English. This does not mean every work has a bilingual edition, or that we cover every language. Helping more people read in a familiar language is an ongoing direction.

Use it. Legibility on a phone, text enlargement, keyboard and screen-reader navigation, and slower connections all affect whether a reachable page is actually readable. Each needs implementation and testing. A general claim of accessibility cannot replace that work.

Reading for everyone is our goal. What remains unfinished should stay visible in our improvement work, not disappear into a promise.

Quality and judgment

Lower prices should not mean a greater burden of checking

If a book is easy to obtain but leaves a reader spending more time identifying errors, the cost has changed form rather than disappeared.

Free access must therefore sit alongside clear quality information. Readers should know how a work was produced, what a score means, what still needs checking and how to report a problem.

In nonfiction, specific facts, quotations and key conclusions deserve scrutiny. In fiction, narrative completeness and internal consistency matter too. “AI-written” is not an excuse for poor work, and a score is not proof that problems have been resolved.

Sharing and rights

One act of creation can serve more than one reader

Carefully organized material may save someone else a period of searching. A different explanation may reveal a perspective they had overlooked. Public sharing matters through these particular forms of help.

Sharing must also respect choice and rights. Authors decide whether to publish. Free reading does not authorize republication, adaptation or redistribution without permission.

We want more people to take part in creating and sharing knowledge, with respect for the work and rights of those who contribute.
