About this book

This definitive history examines the 1956 Suez Crisis as the pivotal moment that dismantled British imperial power and reshaped the modern Middle East. Tracing the arc from Gamal Abdel Nasser’s nationalization of the canal to the secret Anglo-French-Israeli collusion at Sèvres, the narrative details the military invasion and subsequent diplomatic humiliation that redrew the global balance of power. Beyond the immediate conflict, the book explores the broader trajectory of Pan-Arabism, analyzing the rise and fall of the United Arab Republic and Nasser’s enduring legacy through 1970. By connecting the tactical failures at Suez to long-term geopolitical shifts, this work illuminates how a regional dispute accelerated decolonization and established new paradigms in international relations. It is an essential account for understanding the transition from European hegemony to a multipolar world order and the complex roots of contemporary Middle Eastern politics.
