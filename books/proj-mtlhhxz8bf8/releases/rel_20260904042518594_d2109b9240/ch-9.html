The cold metal of the doorknob stung Nora’s palm as she pushed open the heavy glass door of the precinct. The air inside was a wall of stale coffee, industrial cleaner, and the faint, persistent smell of sweat and desperation. She clutched the manila envelope against her chest, her fingers clamped so tightly around it that the tips had gone numb and white. Inside were the photocopied pages—the final, damning entries from Eleanor Lowell’s diary, and the fire department’s incident report with its contradictory timestamps circled in red ink. Last night, after reading Eleanor’s final words—I’m going to ask him tonight. Face to face. I’ve set the recorder. If anything happens to me…—the pressure in Nora’s skull had become a physical thing, a vise threatening to crack her professional composure wide open. She’d spent the hours before dawn pacing her apartment, the ghost of James Lowell’s calm, rehearsed voice in her ear (a perfect, tragic accident) warring with Eleanor’s frantic script. The choice had crystallized with the first grey light: continue her illegal, vulnerable solo hunt for Eleanor’s hidden recorder, or bring everything she had to the one place that could officially turn the gears. She chose the gears, even if they would grind her to dust in the process.

The uniformed officer at the front desk looked up, his expression shifting from bored to politely wary as he took in her tense posture, the professional blazer worn like armor over a hollow core. “Can I help you?”
