初次会谈

The door to the consultation room was tapped with a soft, precise knock. Nora Voss looked up from her notepad to see a man standing in the doorway, framed by the muted hallway light. He was dressed in a charcoal-grey suit that fit him too well for a man who had just lost his wife, his dark hair combed back with a severity that spoke of control, not neglect. His face was the picture of composed sorrow—grief ironed flat, every crease of pain meticulously smoothed into an expression of dignified loss. It was a performance, Nora thought immediately, and the thought left a sour taste at the back of her throat. She had seen many forms of grief in this room: the raw, screaming kind; the numb, hollowed-out silence; the guilty, frantic activity. But this? This felt like a rehearsed soliloquy waiting for its cue.

“Mr. Lowell,” she said, her voice calibrated， to the room’s soft acoustics. “Please, come in.”。

James Lowell entered with a measured step,， his eyes scanning the room—the two armchairs， angled toward each other, the bookshelf of， clinical texts, the single abstract painting， on the neutral wall—before settling on her.， He gave a small, tight nod. “Dr. Voss. Thank， you for seeing me on such short notice.” His， voice was a low baritone, carefully modulated，. He carried the faint, clean scent of sandalwood， and something else—a hint of antiseptic,， perhaps, or laundry starch.。

“Of course. Please, have a seat.” Nora gestured， to the armchair opposite hers, its upholstery， a soft, neutral taupe designed to be unobtrusive，. She watched as he sat, his movements economical，. He placed his hands on his knees, fingers interlaced.。
