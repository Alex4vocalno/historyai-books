The blade of the letter opener sliced through， the packing tape with a soft riiiip, sharp， and clear in the quiet of the apartment.。

Nora Voss let the opener clatter onto the， wooden desk. Her office, two hours ago, had， felt too exposed, too professional. The brown， parcel, left on her desk with no sender, no， note, just her name typed on a plain label,， had hummed with a private, insistent energy，. She couldn’t open it there. So she’d brought， it home, to the sanctuary of her study, where， the only light came from the green-shaded， desk lamp and the only audience was the ghost， of her own professional failure.。

James Lowell’s face floated behind her eyes. His performance in her consultation room yesterday had been flawless. The grief, tailored. The story, smooth. A perfect, tragic accident. His words. His precise, rehearsed phrase. When she’d asked her standard question — “Where do you feel that memory in your body?” — he’d placed a hand over his heart without hesitation. “A hollow ache. Right here. It’s… it’s always there.” But his eyes had been checking her reaction, calibrating, not remembering.

The diary she’d glimpsed in the office — Eleanor’s diary — had been a physical contradiction to that smoothness. The mere sight of its leather cover had made Nora’s throat tighten with a mix of dread and illicit curiosity. Who sent it? Why to her? The questions had buzzed in her skull all through the train ride home, a counterpoint to the memory of James’s controlled baritone describing candlelit dinners and quiet understanding.

She pushed aside the brown paper. Beneath it， was a simple cardboard box. No padding. Just， an object resting inside.。

She reached in and lifted it out.

It was a journal, bound in dark green leather，, worn soft at the corners. The moment it， cleared the box, a smell hit her — acrid,， chemical, cutting through the neutral scents， of paper and dust and her own apartment. It， wasn’t the smell of old paper or ink. It was， sharper, like ammonia with a sour, metallic， edge. It stung the back of her throat faintly.。
