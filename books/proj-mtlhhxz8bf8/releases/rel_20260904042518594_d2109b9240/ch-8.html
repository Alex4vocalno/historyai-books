The door clicked shut behind James Lowell,， and Nora Voss slumped into her desk chair,， her hands trembling so violently she had to， press them flat against the cool wood to， still them. The echo of his threat hung in， the air, thick as smoke. A formal complaint，. Your past failure. Your license. He hadn’t， shouted. He hadn’t needed to. The precision， of his words, the cold clarity in his eyes,， had been far more effective than any rage.， He knew exactly where to cut.。

Not again. You cannot be wrong about this again.。

The thought was a hammer blow to her sternum. She forced air into her lungs, a ragged, painful gasp. The war had indeed begun the moment she’d decided to test him, to ask about the specifics of the fire that didn’t match Eleanor’s diary. But she’d expected defensive bluster, guilty evasion. Not this surgical, pre-emptive strike aimed at the very foundation of her professional existence. He hadn’t denied anything; he’d simply rendered her powerless to pursue it.

The afternoon light, which had been a sharp,， accusing blade when James was here, was now， softening into a melancholy gold, pooling in， the corners of her consultation room. It， highlighted the dust motes dancing in the， silence, the empty client chair still bearing， the faint impression of his weight. Her gaze， fell to the locked drawer of her desk. Inside，, wrapped in a plain manila folder, was Eleanor， Lowell’s diary. Her only weapon. And it was，, so far, a weapon of whispers and contradictions，, not of facts.。

She’d pored over the entries for weeks. The， growing fear, the isolation, the references， to James’s unexplained absences, the perfume， scent on his clothes that wasn’t hers. The， locked window he claimed she always kept， locked. The anniversary dinner he claimed， was a surprise, while she wrote of her dread， for the approaching ‘night of lies.’ But it， was all circumstantial, the anguished， outpourings of an unhappy woman. It proved， James was a liar, but not a murderer. It， wasn’t enough. Not against the threat he now。

The threat he now represented hung in the， silent office air, a palpable chill. Nora’s， fingers, resting on the cool wood of the desk，, twitched. The diary was a scream trapped in， paper and ink, a scream the law refused to， hear. But Eleanor Lowell had been a woman， who planned her garden years in advance, who， wrote of symbiosis and root systems with the， precision of a strategist. Her fear was real，, but so was her cunning.。

Nora pulled the manila folder from the drawer. She didn’t open it to the latest, most desperate entries again; she flipped to the back pages, to the strange, recurring motif that had initially seemed like a nervous doodle, a floral tic: a simple, stylized rose, entwined with sharp, angular thorns. It appeared in margins, at the end of paragraphs, sometimes tiny, sometimes sprawling across half a page. Beneath one particularly dark cluster, Eleanor had written: A rose without a thorn is just a pretty lie. My life has become a garden of such lies.

The phrase ‘pretty lie’ echoed. The anniversary， dinner James claimed was a surprise—the ‘，night of lies.’ The connection was a live wire，. Nora’s eyes scanned earlier entries.
