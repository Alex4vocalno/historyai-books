第 10 章

双火交叠

The cold autumn wind bit into Nora’s face as she stepped out of the police station, the taste of her own confession still metallic on her tongue. She had just spent two hours with Detective Bryson, laying out everything—the diary, her suspicions, her illegal visit to the Lowell house. The gears of the investigation were finally turning, greased by her sacrifice. Her phone buzzed in her pocket. It was a text from her clinical supervisor: Hearing scheduled with the state board. Next Thursday, 10 AM. She stared at the screen, then at the single missed call notification from ‘James Lowell.’ For the first time in weeks, she felt no fear, only a deep, weary calm. She had jumped into the machinery; now she would see what it crushed.

Three days later, she was back in the same， station, sitting in a small, airless conference， room across from Detective Bryson. The room， smelled of stale coffee and industrial cleaner，. On the table between them lay Eleanor Lowell’s， leather-bound diary, sealed in a clear evidence， bag, and a smaller bag containing a cotton swab.。

“You understand the position this puts us in, Ms. Voss,” Bryson said, his voice flat. He was a man in his fifties with a face that had settled into permanent skepticism. “Your testimony is crucial, but your methods… obtaining client records without consent, entering a sealed scene.”

“I understand,” Nora said. Her left thumb， found the scar on its pad and began to rub， it absently. She forced her hand still,， placing it flat on the table. “The diary is， the key. James gave it to me willingly as， part of our therapy.”。

“And this?” Bryson nudged the smaller bag with his pen.

“A scent sample. From the diary’s pages.” Nora， leaned forward slightly. “From our first， session, I noticed it. A faint, acrid smell，. Not paper, not ink. I couldn’t place it then，.” She met his eyes. “I think it’s a chemical， residue. Possibly an accelerant.”。

Bryson’s eyebrow twitched almost imperceptibly. “You think Mrs. Lowell got something on her diary?”

“No.” Nora shook her head slowly. “I think someone else handled it after the fire. Someone who was in that burned room before the investigators arrived.” She tapped the evidence bag over the diary’s cover. “James told me he found this diary in her studio office, untouched by the flames. But that smell… it’s embedded in the paper fibers. It didn’t just waft onto it.”

The detective was silent for a long moment,， his gaze shifting from Nora to the diary and， back. The hum of the fluorescent lights filled， the space.。

“You also mentioned contradictions,” he finally said.。

Nora opened her own folder and slid out two， photocopies side by side. One was from， Eleanor’s diary—a recent entry describing， how she loved to sleep with the bedroom window， open a crack for fresh air, even in autumn.， The other was a transcript excerpt from Nora’s， first recorded session with James.。

“In our initial consultation,” Nora said, her voice taking on the measured cadence of an explanation she had rehearsed mentally a hundred times, “James Lowell stated that his wife was ‘meticulous about security.’ He specifically said she always locked their bedroom window at night.” She pointed to the transcript line: ‘She was safety-conscious… always kept that window locked.’ “He built this detail into his narrative of a tragic accident—a faulty space heater too close to curtains behind a locked window, creating a fatal trap.”

She then pointed to Eleanor’s entry: ‘The night air is so clean here… I keep my window open just a sliver to breathe it in.’
